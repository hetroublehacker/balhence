The practice

Curious about how
things really work.

Balhence is an AI-native security practice working across penetration testing, research, code review, and defensive engineering. The job is to understand your systems well enough to find the problems that matter and explain what to do about them.

That might mean following a permission through a web app and its API, reviewing cloud access, or checking what an AI agent is allowed to do. Mobile apps, infrastructure, identity, source code, and detection are part of the picture too.

AI helps with research, analysis, and building tools. It can also be wrong. A person checks the evidence, reviews the work, and takes responsibility for what we deliver.

If your project needs specialist skills or equipment, we'll say so early. We agree on what we can take on before making a commitment.

Working standards

The basics we don't skip.

01 / SCOPE

Permission comes first.

Systems, access, methods, data handling, contacts, and stop conditions are agreed before work starts.

02 / PROOF

Show the evidence.

A plausible tool result isn't enough. We check findings and changes before asking your team to act on them.

03 / CARE

Use the smallest safe check.

Controlled accounts and synthetic records help us answer the question without touching more data than necessary.

04 / HANDOFF

Make time for questions.

The handover includes a walkthrough and whatever validation or re-testing is agreed in your scope.

Your data and AI

AI use is documented in the scope and rules of engagement. No public model receives client credentials, tokens, customer records, source code, or unredacted vulnerability evidence unless the client explicitly approves the provider, purpose, and processing terms. No model autonomously tests production.

Penetration testing methodology and working standards

We use recognized references to organize coverage and explain the limits of an assessment. The methods used depend on the systems and the agreed scope.

  • Web and API: OWASP WSTG, OWASP Top 10, and OWASP API Security Top 10.
  • AI applications: OWASP LLM Top 10 and OWASP Agentic Applications Top 10.
  • Assessment planning: NIST SP 800-115 and PTES.
  • Technique context, where relevant: MITRE ATT&CK.

These are methodology references, not certifications or endorsements. Every reported finding is reviewed by a person, and the report identifies what was checked, excluded, or left unverified.

For features you build with models or agents, see AI and LLM penetration testing.

Balhence / Santulan

Security has to work with your product.

The name Balhence comes from santulan, meaning balance. Your team has releases to ship, customers to support, and data to protect. Security advice needs to account for all of that.

A recommendation that ignores the way your team works is unlikely to stick. We'll explain the risk, talk through the tradeoffs, and help you choose a fix you can maintain.

Publication and evidence standards

Balhence publishes buyer guides to help teams plan assessments and understand deliverables. Organization bylines link to this approach. A guide describes how to prepare for a service; it does not establish an individual's certification or a client's results.

  • Sources and dates: methodology and regulatory references link to the responsible publishers. Publication dates identify the original article; modification dates reflect substantive content changes.
  • Examples: worksheets and the sample report use fictional systems and findings. They demonstrate scope and report format, not client outcomes.
  • Research: field notes are anonymized. Illustrative code and defensive checks are labeled separately from observations.
  • Corrections: send a page URL, the statement, and a supporting source to contact@balhence.com. Please exclude credentials and confidential findings.

Start with the penetration testing guides or compare assessment types and their limits.
