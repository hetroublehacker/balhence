About Balhence

Good security starts
with good questions.

What could go wrong? How would you know? What would it take to fix? We help you work through those questions in your product, your code, and the systems behind them.

The practice

Curious about how
things really work.

Balhence is an AI-native security practice working across penetration testing, research, code review, and defensive engineering. The job is to understand your systems well enough to find the problems that matter and explain what to do about them.

That might mean following a permission through a web app and its API, reviewing cloud access, or checking what an AI agent is allowed to do. Mobile apps, infrastructure, identity, source code, and detection are part of the picture too.

AI helps with research, analysis, and building tools. It can also be wrong. A person checks the evidence, reviews the work, and takes responsibility for what we deliver.

If your project needs specialist skills or equipment, we'll say so early. We agree on what we can take on before making a commitment.

Working standards

The basics we don't skip.

01 / SCOPE

Permission comes first.

Systems, access, methods, data handling, contacts, and stop conditions are agreed before work starts.

02 / PROOF

Show the evidence.

A plausible tool result isn't enough. We check findings and changes before asking your team to act on them.

03 / CARE

Use the smallest safe check.

Controlled accounts and synthetic records help us answer the question without touching more data than necessary.

04 / HANDOFF

Make time for questions.

The handover includes a walkthrough and whatever validation or re-testing is agreed in your scope.

How we use AI

Useful tools.
Our responsibility.

We use AI for research, analysis, code, and documentation where it helps the work. Your approval determines which tools and data we can use. Human review stays part of the process.

Where AI fits

  • Researching technologies and organizing approved project context
  • Connecting observations across code, configuration, and workflows
  • Helping review threats, controls, and detection coverage
  • Developing custom defensive tools and repeatable checks
  • Organizing redacted evidence with traceable sources
  • Drafting documentation, remediation options, and handover notes

What people own

  • The scope, permissions, and safety of each action
  • Architecture decisions and the tradeoffs behind them
  • Validation of findings and review of generated code
  • The meaning of the evidence and its business impact
  • Recommendations, priorities, and quality of delivery
  • Checking fixes and deliverables against the agreed requirements
Your data and AI

AI use is documented in the scope and rules of engagement. No public model receives client credentials, tokens, customer records, source code, or unredacted vulnerability evidence unless the client explicitly approves the provider, purpose, and processing terms. No model autonomously tests production.

Balhence / Santulan

Security has to work with your product.

The name Balhence comes from santulan, meaning balance. Your team has releases to ship, customers to support, and data to protect. Security advice needs to account for all of that.

A recommendation that ignores the way your team works is unlikely to stick. We'll explain the risk, talk through the tradeoffs, and help you choose a fix you can maintain.

Before you choose

Take a look at the work.

Our sample penetration test report uses fictional systems and findings to show the format. For other projects, we'll agree on the deliverable and how to check it before starting.

Work you can inspect

  • A detailed sample report for penetration testing
  • Methods and deliverables matched to the engagement
  • Clear scope, exclusions, and safety controls
  • Published AI-use and client-data guardrails
  • Practical handover and agreed validation checks
  • Direct access to the person responsible for delivery

Clear expectations

  • The work covers the systems and outcomes we agree on
  • Results reflect the version and access available during the work
  • Every reported issue is checked by a person
  • No assessment can guarantee that every vulnerability has been found
  • Your auditor or reviewer decides whether deliverables meet their needs
  • Testing starts only after written authorization
