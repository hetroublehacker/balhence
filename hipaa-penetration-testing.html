HIPAA penetration testing

HIPAA penetration testing evaluates technical safeguards in applications and systems that handle electronic protected health information. Balhence assesses authentication, permissions, data exposure, and integrations within an agreed scope, using controlled accounts and suitable test data. Findings support your Security Rule risk analysis and remediation planning; a pentest alone does not establish compliance.

How penetration testing supports the HIPAA Security Rule

The Security Rule requires regulated entities to assess risks to ePHI and implement safeguards. A scoped penetration test can provide technical evidence for that risk analysis. It complements the wider administrative, physical, and technical assessment described in HHS guidance.

HHS has proposed explicit annual penetration testing in its Security Rule update. The HHS proposal fact sheet distinguishes the proposed changes from the current rule. Confirm the applicable requirements and effective dates with your compliance adviser when scoping the engagement.

Protecting ePHI during a security assessment

Map where health information enters the product, who can access it, where it is stored, and which integrations receive it. Use synthetic or suitably de-identified records wherever possible. Agree access, evidence redaction, storage, retention, deletion, and incident contacts before testing.

Discuss whether a business associate agreement is needed before access is granted. Do not put patient data or credentials in the enquiry form.

What we test in healthcare applications

  • Web applications and APIs that handle ePHI.
  • Access boundaries between patient, provider, staff, and organization roles.
  • Sign-in, sessions, account recovery, and administrative actions.
  • Data exposure in responses, exports, logs, and errors.
  • Authorized integrations and protections for data in transit.

Plan the application boundary with our web application testing and SaaS tenant-isolation testing services.

Prepare a healthcare application testing scope

Map the patient, provider, staff, and administrative roles across each relevant organization. Identify where ePHI enters, moves through, and leaves the application, including exports and third-party integrations.

Prepare synthetic or suitably de-identified records and controlled accounts. Before sharing sensitive material, agree the permitted access, applicable agreements, evidence location, retention, deletion, and incident contacts.

Tell us which technical questions your risk analysis needs to answer. Keep the assessment's findings and limitations alongside the broader risk register so the compliance team can make informed treatment decisions. Use these questions to plan your healthcare application assessment.

Findings for your HIPAA risk analysis

  • An executive summary, agreed scope, dates, methodology, and coverage limits.
  • Human-verified findings with impact, prerequisites, severity reasoning, practical fixes, and evidence redacted according to agreed data-handling rules.
  • A report walkthrough and an agreed route for urgent findings.
  • A bounded re-test of agreed fixes when included in the agreement, with each finding recorded as fixed, partially fixed, not fixed, or not re-tested.
  • The synthetic or suitably de-identified data used, affected role boundaries, and limits on evidence collection, so your compliance team can interpret the findings.

We describe what the technical assessment covered and what remains outside it. Your compliance team owns the broader risk analysis, risk treatment, and regulatory decisions.

HIPAA penetration testing FAQ

Does a pentest make us HIPAA compliant?

No. It evaluates agreed technical safeguards and supplies evidence for a wider compliance program. It does not replace the required risk analysis or other administrative, physical, and technical safeguards.

Do you sign a BAA?

Discuss the proposed access and data handling during scoping. We agree applicable terms before testing and prefer synthetic or suitably de-identified data to minimize exposure to ePHI.

Can our patient-facing SaaS product be included?

Yes, subject to agreed scope and access. Include the relevant roles, organizations, APIs, integrations, and data flows; testing begins only with written authorization.
