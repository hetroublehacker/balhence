PTaaS vs an annual penetration test: which fits?

Choose the testing cadence from what changes in your product and what evidence you need. The delivery model should make the coverage easier to understand.

By Balhence22 September 2026

When a one-time penetration test fits

A defined assessment window works well for a particular launch, customer review, or audit milestone. The proposal can fix the system boundary, access, deliverables, and re-test window before work begins.

The report describes the systems at the time they were tested. Later releases do not inherit that assurance automatically. Keep a record of significant changes and decide whether they need an additional test.

When PTaaS or continuous testing fits

Recurring work can suit a product that frequently changes roles, permissions, integrations, or sensitive workflows. Start with a baseline, then choose the scope for each cycle from the changes and remaining risks.

At Balhence, penetration testing as a service means scheduled testing with people doing the work, an updated finding register, and agreed re-tests. It does not imply constant coverage or a real-time self-service dashboard.

Questions to ask when comparing testing proposals
DecisionWhat to confirm
CoverageDoes each cycle cover the full application or only selected changes?
CadenceWhat starts a cycle, how much work is reserved, and how is availability agreed?
EvidenceCan reviewers see test dates, versions, findings, exclusions, and status history?
Re-testsWhich fixes are eligible, how many checks are included, and when do they expire?
OwnershipWho approves scope changes, receives urgent findings, and owns remediation?

Price comparisons need the same assumptions. Use the pentest cost and scope guide to compare the actual work.

Keep audit and customer commitments visible

A recurring series of narrow tests may leave parts of the audit boundary untouched. An annual assessment can also miss important changes made after its report. Map both models to the coverage and dates promised in your controls or contracts.

For SOC 2 penetration testing, agree the evidence with the auditor. For other frameworks, verify the applicable testing frequency and scope with the responsible reviewer. PTaaS is a delivery model, not a compliance exemption.

Start with a baseline and a change record

List the features expected to change, how often sensitive releases occur, and the decisions the testing must support. Decide whether a focused follow-up, a recurring cadence, or another full assessment best addresses those changes.

If you need a starting point, create a private scope draft and read the SaaS readiness checklist. We can agree the baseline first and discuss recurring work after the scope is clear.
